Biography
Yang Feng
is an associate professor and Ph.D. supervisor at
Nanjing University
. His research focuses on two directions:
software quality assurance and intelligent technologies
, and
language-based software engineering
.
Software quality assurance and intelligent technologies
He develops testing and debugging techniques to improve the reliability and robustness of complex software and AI systems. Representative work includes
DeepGini
(ISSTA 2020) for prioritizing tests for deep neural networks,
QATest
(ASE 2022) for fuzzing question-answering systems, and
RediI
(ICSE 2025) for deterministic failure reproduction in distributed systems. His work also includes
TACO
(ICSE 2026) for assessing the trustworthiness of large language models in coding assistance, and
AtPatch
(ICSE 2026) for debugging Transformer models by correcting anomalous attention during inference. His recent research further investigates the behavioral trustworthiness and security of large language models and agents.
Language-based software engineering
He develops techniques and tools for improving the reliability of Rust software and compilers, and studies trustworthy C/C++-to-Rust translation. His work includes
FRIES
for fuzzing Rust library interactions,
PALM
for improving Rust unit test coverage, and
RustGTC
for constraint-guided API test generation. He also investigates code–comment inconsistency detection through
RustC4
/
RustC4++
, compiler fix-suggestion testing through
SugBreaker
(FSE 2026), and panic-bug analysis and repair through
PanicFI
. His research has received
multiple Distinguished Paper Awards
at ASE 2022, ESEC/FSE 2023, and Internetware 2023.
His work has also earned
strong recognition from industry and open-source communities
. His honors include
Huawei’s Spark Award
and
Innovation Practice Award
, the
Xiaomi Youth Science and Technology Award
, and the
Xuanwu Community Outstanding Contribution Award
. He was also recognized as a
2024 Annual Partner
by Ant Group’s TuGraph community.

Research
Yang Feng’s team studies how to make complex software and AI systems more reliable, and how to build the tools and infrastructure that support them. The team’s research combines software testing, program analysis, language-based software engineering, and systems development.
Software Quality Assurance and Intelligent Technologies
This direction investigates how to characterize software quality, expose failures, diagnose their causes, and assess the trustworthiness of software and AI systems.
Quality Characterization and Adaptive Testing
When a system has countless possible inputs, which tests are most likely to reveal problems?
This topic studies how quality measures and testing evidence can guide test generation, selection, and prioritization.
DeepGini
(ISSTA 2020) investigates test prioritization for deep neural networks, while
Adaptive Test Selection
(ICSE 2022) explores how to adapt test selection for these systems. For question-answering systems,
QATest
(ASE 2022) provides a uniform fuzzing framework.
Multi-Source Defect Diagnosis and Robustness Enhancement
When an AI system makes a mistake, does the problem come from its data, its model, or the conditions in which it operates?
This topic investigates different sources of failure and develops techniques for diagnosis and robustness testing. At the data level,
Datactive
(ISSTA 2024) localizes data faults in object detection systems. At the model level,
AtPatch
(ICSE 2026) diagnoses and corrects anomalous attention in Transformer models. Research on operating conditions includes
Drivence
(TSE 2026), which synthesizes realistic driving sequences to test multi-sensor fusion perception systems.
Trustworthiness and Security of Large Language Models and Agents
When should we trust a model’s answer or an agent’s action?
This topic investigates how to evaluate models and agents in the contexts where they are used.
TACO
(ICSE 2026) studies the trustworthiness of large language models in coding assistance, while
VADA
(ICSE 2026) supports value-aware data generation and alignment evaluation across cultural contexts. The team is also investigating the behavioral trustworthiness and security of agents, including their responses to unsafe or adversarial interactions.
System Testing and Fault Diagnosis for AI Infrastructure
How can we find and diagnose failures in the software that trains and runs AI models?
This topic studies defects in the libraries, frameworks, and distributed systems supporting AI applications. An
empirical study of deep learning framework bugs
(Information and Software Technology 2022) examines their characteristics, providing evidence for understanding infrastructure failures. Building testing techniques for these systems,
MoCo
(TSE 2025) generates tests for deep learning libraries through code assembly. For distributed-system debugging,
RediI
(ICSE 2025) enables deterministic failure reproduction. The team is currently extending its research to testing, fault diagnosis, and failure reproduction in inference services and distributed training systems.
Language-based Software Engineering and AI Infrastructure
This direction investigates how language properties can support software analysis, migration, testing, and repair, alongside the construction of storage and execution infrastructure for AI workloads.
Type Analysis and Compilation Efficiency
How can tools infer useful information about a program, and how can its compilation process become more efficient?
This topic focuses on type analysis and the organization of compilation workflows. The team is currently working on type analysis for dynamically typed programs, together with techniques for reusing compilation results and coordinating distributed compilation tasks. These efforts aim to improve the information available to development tools and reduce the overhead of building software.
Semantic Preservation and Constraint Adaptation in Cross-Language Translation
How can we migrate C/C++ software to Rust while preserving its intended behavior?
Moving between languages requires addressing differences in types, memory management, ownership, and interfaces. The team is currently working on trustworthy C/C++-to-Rust translation, investigating how to preserve program semantics while adapting to Rust’s language constraints. A central concern is how to assess the correctness of translated programs and support practical migration with explicit attention to semantic preservation.
Language Constraints for Defect Detection and Repair
How can language rules and API contracts help us generate better tests and find bugs?
This topic uses language properties, program context, and API information to guide testing and analysis, with Rust as a central focus.
FRIES
(ISSTA 2024) tests Rust library interactions through ecosystem-guided target generation.
PALM
(ASE 2025) combines program analysis and language models to improve unit-test coverage, while
RustGTC
(ASE 2026, accepted) investigates constraint-guided API test generation using comments and code context.
The research also addresses consistency checking, repair, and compiler diagnostics.
RustC4++
(TOSEM 2026) detects inconsistencies between Rust code and comments, and
PanicFI
(TOSEM 2026) provides infrastructure for fixing panic bugs. At the toolchain level,
SugBreaker
(FSE 2026) tests the reliability of Rust compiler fix suggestions through constraint-violation-guided mutation.
Distributed File Systems and Heterogeneous Engines for AI
How can distributed storage and heterogeneous engines better support AI workloads?
The team is currently working on the design and implementation of distributed file systems and heterogeneous engines for AI. The file-system work addresses the storage and data-access needs of AI workloads, while the engine work concerns execution support in heterogeneous environments. This research brings together storage-system design and execution-engine development to provide efficient and reliable infrastructure for AI applications.
